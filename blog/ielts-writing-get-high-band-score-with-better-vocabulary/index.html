IELTS Writing – Get High Band Score with Better Vocabulary

By casperblog_admin 6 March 2023 2 min read

All the IELTS aspirants will be looking for one or other way to get high IELTS band scores in the first attempt itself. Knowing such tips and tricks will help you in facing the exams easily. Casper Academy of Excellence is the leading institute to give IELTS coaching in Kochi.

The Difficult Part

The task response, coherence and cohesiveness, lexical resource, and grammatical range and accuracy are the four primary areas that the examiner will consider while grading your IELTS Writing problems (check out the detailed band descriptors for IELTS Writing). You can see from the lexical resource that a portion of your IELTS Writing band score will be determined by the variety of language you employ. Make every word matter because you only have 150 words in Part 1 and 250 words in Part 2 to do this. Examinators check to see if an applicant:

  • Employs a sufficient vocabulary for the purpose.(Band 6)
  • Employs a wide enough vocabulary to allow for some flexibility and accuracy. (Band 7)
  • This conveys accurate meanings using a wide variety of language fluently and flexibly (Band 8-9)

In this article, we’ll look at some ways to increase your vocabulary and demonstrate your knowledge to the examiner.

The answer

Expanding your vocabulary is the first step. A excellent place to start is through reading. You can start by reading books about IELTS, news articles, books that interest you, or even vocabulary-building apps. Instead of focusing on temporary fixes, you will instead be enhancing your overall reading ability, which will help you in the long run. But that just tells half the tale. Being succinct is crucial for both of your writing assignments. Casper Academy of Excellence is bringing best results for students by giving excellent IELTS coaching in Ernakulam.

Doing this is frequently simple. Replace each of the words in the example below with a single word (solutions are provided below).

  • at this moment in time
  • in spite of the fact that
  • smaller in size
  • red in colour
  • few in number
  • in the event that
  • is allowed to
  • last but not least
  • with the exception of
  • at all times

Next step

When you have completed the exercise above and checked your answers, go back to a practice essay you have written recently. Look at each sentence and see how many words you can remove while still expressing the same idea. You may need to rewrite the sentence, or simply remove some unnecessary (or ‘filler’) words.
