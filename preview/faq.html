Frequently Asked Questions

Everything students usually ask us about the IELTS test. Can't find your question? Ask us.

Why should I score good in IELTS exam?
Worldwide, corporations have confidence in IELTS results. Your IELTS band scores determine your degree of English proficiency. It measures your English speaking, listening, reading, and writing comprehension. If you have a score of seven or more, you are deemed a good user.

Where can I test IELTS?
The IELTS exam can now be taken on both a computer and on paper. Those who choose to take the IELTS on paper will complete the Writing, Listening, and Reading sections with a pen or HB pencil on paper. The Speaking test is given in person or via video call by a trained IELTS examiner. After 13 calendar days, the results of the paper-based IELTS examination are released.
Those who opt for computer-based IELTS complete the listening, reading, and writing sections on a computer. The Speaking test is still given in person or via video call with an IELTS-trained examiner. Computer-based IELTS allows you to choose from multiple test dates and times, and you can expect your results within three to five days.

How do I book or register for the test?
First, there are over 1,600 IELTS test centers in over 140 countries. Locate the nearest testing centre.
You can now take the IELTS Academic test from the comfort of your own home or another location of your choice with IELTS Online, our online IELTS Academic test.
Step 2: You can register and pay online, or you can download an application form to print, complete, and submit to the testing centre of your choosing.
You must present a copy of your valid identification document. You must also present the same identification on the day of your exam. Step 3: Your IELTS exam is scheduled

How much does it cost?

The IELTS academic exam fee and the IELTS general exam fee are identically priced in India at ₹19,000 (from April 2026). Fees change from time to time, so check the current fee on the official IDP or British Council booking page before you register.

What are the test rules?
The duration of the listening, reading, and writing tests is 2 hours and 40 minutes, and there are no breaks between sections. Ensure that you are well-prepared and that you have something to eat and drink beforehand. You are not permitted to bring food into the testing room. You may only consume beverages in transparent bottles.

Do I take all parts of the tests on the same day? Which part will I take first?
The listening test will be administered first, followed by the reading and writing sections. Depending on the test centre, the Speaking test may be administered on the same day or up to three days before or after the test date.

What if I need to postpone or cancel my IELTS test?
You may cancel your IELTS test registration at any time prior to taking the exam by contacting the Test Center. The terms of refunds depend on the date of cancellation and the existence of exceptional circumstances.

What is the minimum age requirement for IELTS?
IELTS is a test designed to evaluate an individual's English language proficiency. Therefore, the examination can be taken at any age. However, the minimum age required to take the exam is 16.

What can I take into the examination room?
You may only keep a pencil, a pen, your identification card, and an eraser on your desk.

What is IELTS Speaking test? What I should know to score good in Speaking test?
IELTS Speaking is a face-to-face, informal conversation with an IELTS examiner for both academic and general training. The exam consists of three sections designed to assess your pronunciation, fluency, grammar, and vocabulary.
Tips for improving your performance on the speaking portion of the IELTS examination
  • Try to unwind
  • Extend your responses.
  • Make the most of your sixty seconds.
  • Do not memorise
  • Speak with clarity and a natural rate.
  • Avoid giving up!
  • Keep a journal.
  • Know what to anticipate.

What is IELTS Writing test? What I should know to score good in Writing test?
  • The writing exam consists of two tasks: Task 1 and Task 2.
  • Task 1 requires a minimum of 150 words, while Task 2 requires a minimum of 250 words.
  • Task 1 should take roughly 20 minutes, while Task 2 should take roughly 40 minutes
  • Task 2 contributes twice as many points to the final grade as does Task 1.
  • Academic and General Training versions of the IELTS writing test are available.
    Task 2 is identical on both exams, while Task 1 is distinct. The main difference is in how the question is asked and what kind of language is needed to answer it.
Academic Writing
  • Task 1: One of the following will be presented to you:
    • Bar graph
    • Line graph
    • Table
    • Pie chart
    • Process diagram
    • Map
    • Combination of graphs, tables, and charts You must explain, summarise, or describe the information in your own words.
    • You may be asked, for instance, to describe and explain data.
      • elucidate the steps of a procedure
      • explain how something operates
      • describe a thing or an occurrence.
  • Task 2 requires you to compose an essay on a subject of general interest. The essay will be a response to a particular viewpoint, problem, or argument stated in the question. Both of your responses should be written in a formal tone.
General Writing
  • Task 1: You must compose a letter requesting information or explaining a specific circumstance.
    - The letter must conform to one of the following formats, as applicable:
    - formal—when speaking to a stranger. informal—to a close friend or relative.
  • Task 2 requires you to compose an essay on a topic of general interest. Your essay will be a response to a particular viewpoint, problem, or argument stated in the question.
    - You can be more personal than you have to be in Academic Writing Task 2 essays.

What is IELTS Reading test? What I should know to score good in Reading test?
The reading test will assess several abilities, including:
  • Creating a diagram, table, or synopsis
  • Differentiate between main ideas and supporting details.
  • Find specific information
  • Determine the author's viewpoint.
  • Following major arguments
  • Determine the writer's intent.

IELTS Reading Tips
  • Do not assume that you will comprehend every word.
  • Practice your reading abilities, not your IELTS abilities.
  • Do not panic.
  • It is actually a test of vocabulary.
  • Timing is essential.
  • Ignore any prior knowledge on the subject.
  • Practice slowly and quickly.
  • Be cautious when transferring your responses.
  • Leave no blanks.

What is IELTS Listening test? What I should know to score good in Listening test?
Before the recording begins, you will have time to review the instructions and questions. Again, as you read the questions, highlight the keywords and try to anticipate the context of the recordings—who will be speaking and the topic of the speech.
Six techniques to improve your listening
  • Again, remember the test!
  • Practice, practise, practise.
  • It all comes down to keywords.
  • Avoid distractions.
  • Do not wait too long for the response.
  • Transfer your answers with care.

What kinds of accents can be heard in Listening and Speaking tests?
IELTS Listening uses a variety of native English accents, including British English, North American English, Australian/New Zealand/South African English, and Irish English. Before taking the IELTS, you should make sure you are used to hearing all of these different kinds of English.

Can I use capital letters?
We recommend writing all of your Reading and Listening answers in all capital letters so that the examiner can easily understand them. In the computer-delivered IELTS, however, handwriting is not a concern, so you should attempt to write all words in upper and lower case.

How are the tests marked?
Each correct response is worth one point. Scores out of forty are converted to the nine-band IELTS scale. Whole and half bands are used to report scores. Academic and general training reading tests use the same grading scale.
The IELTS Speaking test is evaluated in a face-to-face interview by certified IELTS examiners using a set of assessment criteria to award band scores for each of the four criteria: fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation.
The IELTS Writing test is evaluated in the same manner for both Writing Task 1 and Writing Task 2, with examiners awarding band scores for each of the four criteria that correspond most closely to the performance at each band level.

Do I have to write in pencil?
On the writing exam, all responses must be written with a pen or pencil. You have the option of writing in all capital letters or all lowercase letters. You are permitted to make notes on the exam paper, but keep in mind that anything written on the paper will not be graded.

When will I receive my test results?
The IELTS paper test results will be released 13 days after the written examination. The IELTS computer-based test results will be available three to five days after the exam.

How many test report forms (TRFs) am I entitled to?
The TRF, or Test Report Form, is a copy of your IELTS score. Each candidate receives a single IELTS TRF that is valid for two years. In the event that you are applying to Citizenship and Immigration Canada (CIC) or the United Kingdom Border Agency (UKBA), you will receive two copies of your TRF. To obtain these two copies, you must provide CIC and UKBA with proof of application.
The institution(s) you list on your IELTS application form will receive up to five copies of your ATRF. While standard postage is free, you may be charged for courier services and/or international mailing.

How soon can I re-sit the test?
You can take the IELTS again as soon as you feel ready if you didn't get the score you wanted

What topics are covered in IELTS?
IELTS consists of four sections: listening, reading, writing, and speaking. Two distinct IELTS modules exist: academic and general training. In both modules, the speaking and listening tests are identical, but the reading and writing tests are distinct.

Does IELTS include American English?
You may use whichever English language is most comfortable for you. You can choose between British English and American English. IELTS examiners will award you the same score regardless of whether you use American or British English, as both are regarded as acceptable forms of the language.

How long does it take to prepare to get a score of 8 7 7 7 in the IELTS?
Current English Proficiency: If you already have a strong foundation in English and are comfortable with reading, writing, listening, and speaking, it might take you a shorter time to prepare. In this case, intensive preparation for a few months might be sufficient.
Regular Language Use: If you have been regularly using English in your daily life, such as reading books, watching movies, listening to songs, and engaging in conversations, you may need less time to prepare. It's essential to maintain consistent exposure to the language.
Dedication and Study Time: Your dedication and the number of hours you can devote to IELTS preparation per day or week will also impact how long it takes. If you can study intensively for several hours each day, you might progress more quickly.
Practice Tests: Taking practice tests is crucial to familiarize yourself with the IELTS format and timing. Regularly practicing under test conditions can help you improve faster.

Which is better Online or Offline IELTS?
Both the General Training and Academic modules of the IELTS can be taken on a computer or on paper. The test format, question types, time allotted to each test section, and content are identical for both the IELTS computer-based test and the IELTS paper-based test. The only difference is your experience on test day.

Which centre should I choose for the test?
It is a common misconception that IELTS is all about a few-month course, and if you can't pass, you should switch tutors or institutions. That will not assist you either. IELTS is based on your command of the English language, so there is no wrong place for you to take the exam if you have a sufficient grasp of the language

How difficult is writing in the IELTS test?
The fact that many test-takers struggle to pass the writing section is not unusual given that the average score is six bands. Numerous candidates have attempted the examination multiple times but have failed despite their preparation. This is due to the fact that the test-takers do not meet the criteria. They consistently miss the criteria, which is the target, and fail the writing section as a result.
